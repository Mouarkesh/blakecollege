I. Historical Lineage: William Blake → Ray Peat (1962 / 1966)

Blake College Press revives the anti-dogmatic educational and publishing lineage of poet-engraver William Blake (1757–1827) and biologist-educator Ray Peat (1936–2022). Disillusioned with compartmentalized, credential-driven academic bureaucracy, Peat founded the original Blake College in Mexico City in 1962 (relocating to Valle de Bravo) and reopened it in a Victorian house near Skinner’s Butte in Eugene, Oregon in 1966.

Modeled on Blake’s illuminated printing and England’s “free scholars” tradition, Blake College rejected rigid departmental silos to unite Physiology, Philosophy, Mathematics, Poetics & Language, and Ecology under a single motto: “teaching how to invent knowledge and solve problems.”

II. Four Output Artifacts, CC-BY-4.0 & Non-Degree Charter

To prevent any single academic discipline from dominating the press, all work is indexed across four co-equal Output Artifacts: Translations & Editions ([TRN]), Treatises & Monographs ([MON]), Replications & Protocols ([PRT]), and Critiques & Marginalia ([CRT]). Every release is published free of paywalls under Creative Commons Attribution (CC-BY-4.0) licensing with a permanent Zenodo DOI.

Legal & Institutional Disclaimer: Blake College Press is an independent interdisciplinary research guild and open-access publishing house. It is not a state-accredited college or university and does not grant state-accredited academic degrees.
